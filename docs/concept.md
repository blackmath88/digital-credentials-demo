# Concept — Digital Credentials

## Question

What if a University of Basel or Implement training certificate were not just a PDF, but a portable digital credential that participants can understand, keep, share and later verify independently?

## Product thesis

The credential should feel valuable **before** the user knows anything about credential standards. The public artefact is the product surface: calm, legible, printable and useful on LinkedIn, a CV or a personal site. Standards machinery stays underneath it.

The long-term architecture should be standards-oriented rather than proprietary. The target is Open Badges 3.0, which aligns educational achievements with the W3C Verifiable Credentials model. MIT's Digital Credentials Consortium publishes practical Open Badges 3.0 certificate examples that we can use as interoperability fixtures.

## Bite 1

Bite 1 intentionally proves the recipient experience first. The initial credential is a structured digital representation of an existing Implement Learning Institute paper certificate. It is **not** presented as a newly issued or cryptographically verified Implement credential.

The prototype includes a public credential page, A4/print treatment, QR/copy/share actions, a LinkedIn handoff, HTML/CV embed, machine-readable demo record and explicit status language that distinguishes representation from verification.

## Design position

Not a badge dashboard. Not gamification. Not generic SaaS.

The visual language borrows from institutional publishing, Swiss editorial design and the physical authority of a certificate: typographic hierarchy, rules, whitespace, warm paper, near-zero ornament and restrained status signalling.
