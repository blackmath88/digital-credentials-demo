# Higher-education and professional reference cases

Synthesised from the [source report](source-report.md).

## Higher education

| Example | Credential / use case | Maturity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **University of Oxford** | Accredible pilot for **internal professional learning** — including *Confident Manager* and *Project Management Essentials*. Wallet, LinkedIn/CV sharing, and long-term access after leaving Oxford `ox.ac.uk` | Operational pilot; **highly comparable to Basel's proposed use case** |
| **ECIU University / University of Twente** | E-sealed microcredentials issued through **EDC** for challenge-based learning across a European University alliance | Strong EDC reference implementation |
| **SURF edubadges (Netherlands)** | National higher-education badge platform linked to lifelong eduID, with backpack, consent and external authenticity checking `surf.nl` | **Most convincing sector-level infrastructure example** |
| **Imperial Executive Education** | Verified digital completion certificates for executive programmes | Operational certificate channel; exact standard/vendor not stated |
| **MIT Learn / DCC** | Signed OB3 samples, QR links, VerifierPlus and Learner Credential Wallet interoperability `github.com` | Important **reference architecture**, not European production evidence |

### The two cases that matter most to Basel

**Oxford** is the closest analogue. It is internal professional learning, not degree credit; it is explicitly a pilot; and the learner keeps access after leaving the institution. That is almost exactly the shape proposed here.

**SURF edubadges** shows what the mature end looks like — a sector-wide platform tied to a national lifelong identity. It is the destination, not the first step, and it took national coordination that Switzerland does not yet have.

### A caution the report makes explicitly

Public evidence reviewed for **Cambridge, UCL and several German, Nordic, Austrian and French institutions** was often limited to proprietary badges, isolated courses, or projects without exact standards. **These should not be presented as equivalent to SURF or EDC issuance.**

This matters for how the pilot is pitched: the honest claim is "a handful of institutions run this properly and many more are experimenting", not "European higher education has adopted this".

## Professional and corporate learning

| Provider | Verified practice | Portability assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Implement Consulting Group** | **No public standards-level badge/VC programme identified** in the reviewed material | An opportunity — but absence of evidence is not proof that no internal capability exists |
| **Deloitte** | Credly badges with skills, training, assessment and engagement criteria; badges explicitly carry limitations regarding reliance `credly.com` | Shareable vendor-network credential; exact per-badge OB version not shown |
| **PwC** | Credly learning badges encoding hours, application and assessment criteria `credly.com` | Strong capability-evidence design benchmark |
| **Microsoft** | Credly-hosted secure and verifiable badges for certifications and exam-based achievements `credly.com` | Mature professional-brand ecosystem |
| **AWS** | Credly badges, public links, social sharing, and **active/expired certification status tied to recertification** `aws.amazon.com` | Excellent verification and **lifecycle** benchmark |
| **McKinsey Academy, BCG/BCG X, EY, KPMG, Accenture, Capgemini, BearingPoint, IMD** | Learning programmes or certificates found, but insufficient consistent evidence to classify standards, wallet support or portability | Benchmarking leads, **not verified OB3 cases** |

### The lesson the report draws

Not *"consultancies already use OB3 everywhere"* — they do not, and the report could not verify that they do.

The lesson is that major providers use credentials to make **assessment criteria, skills, validity and brand visible**. AWS in particular is called out as a lifecycle benchmark because its credentials carry active/expired status tied to recertification.

And the condition attached to it:

> A Basel–Implement credential adds value beyond marketing only if it remains **downloadable, independently verifiable and governed by transparent achievement criteria**.

That sentence is effectively the pilot's quality bar.

## What this means for Implement

**Evidence:** no public standards-level credential programme was identified for Implement. The report is careful that this is not proof that none exists internally.

**Recommendation:** this is framed as an *opportunity* rather than a gap — Implement has no legacy badge platform to migrate away from, and a Basel-issued credential with Implement as named delivery partner is a cleaner starting point than most consultancies could arrange.
