More than a certificate page

The website makes the certificate desirable. Open Badges makes it portable, verifiable and interoperable.

The page you just scrolled past is a rendering. Underneath it there is a structured credential that can be read by other systems, checked by someone who does not trust this website, and kept by the participant long after this demo is gone.

  1. Human-readable page Typography, print, QR, sharing. Presentation only — it proves nothing on its own.
  2. OpenBadgeCredential Achievement, criteria, competencies, issuer and recipient as structured data. See the mapping in this demo ↗
  3. Cryptographic proof A signature over the exact credential data. Change one field and verification fails. Run the tamper test ↗
  4. Portable learning record The participant keeps it. Other verifiers and wallets can read it without asking us. See the external-verifier handoff ↗
  • HTML is presentation. It decides how the achievement looks, not what it means or whether it is true.
  • Open Badges 3.0 is the education credential model. An open 1EdTech standard for describing what was achieved, by whom, on what criteria.
  • W3C Verifiable Credentials are the trust envelope. They carry the issuer, the validity dates and the proof around that achievement data.
  • Issuer identity plus proof allow independent verification. Someone can check the credential without trusting the site that displays it.
  • One credential, many renderings. The same record can appear as a web page, a printed certificate, a wallet entry, a line on a CV or a row in an HR / L&D system.

The certificate page is one view of the credential. It is not the credential itself.

Why this matters

Three different people get three different things.

The same credential record serves the participant who earned it, the organiser who issued it and the institution that wants learning to be reusable across providers.

Participants

The person who did the learning
  • Keep proof of learning, independent of any inbox or portal
  • Use it on LinkedIn, a CV or a personal website
  • Portable beyond the issuer’s own website
  • Independently verifiable by an employer or a school
  • Potentially stackable into a longer learning pathway

Organisers

The team running the training
  • Define the achievement once, issue it to many participants
  • Standardised metadata instead of free-text certificates
  • Much less manual PDF work per cohort
  • A consistent record of what was issued, to whom, when
  • The participant experience improves without extra effort

Institutions

The organisation setting the direction
  • Reusable learning infrastructure rather than one more certificate tool
  • Internal and external learning described with one model
  • Interoperability instead of vendor lock-in
  • A path towards LMS, HR and learner-record integration
  • A way to recognise learning delivered by partners

The standard underneath

Open, layered, and not invented here.

Nothing in this prototype is a proprietary credential format. Each layer below is public, documented and implemented by other people.

W3C Verifiable CredentialsTrust envelope

Defines how a credential carries its issuer, validity and cryptographic proof so that a third party can check it.

Open Badges 3.0The open standard · 1EdTech

The education-specific model on top of that envelope: achievement, criteria, competencies, recipient, evidence.

Digital Credentials ConsortiumHigher-ed tooling

A university-led group building open-source issuing, wallet and verification tooling for this standard in higher education.

MIT Learn and other university implementationsReference deployments

Working examples of the standard in production, which this prototype uses as interoperability fixtures.

Open Badges 3.0 is the open standard. MIT does not own a separate credential standard. MIT and the Digital Credentials Consortium are reference implementations and ecosystem leaders — which is exactly why their examples are useful to test against.

Strategic direction

From better certificates to a credential ecosystem.

The institutional opportunity is not a nicer certificate website. It is a standards-based way to issue, recognise and combine learning credentials.

LEVEL 1

Better certificates

A PDF becomes a premium digital credential: readable, printable, shareable, with a permanent link.

Demonstrated in this prototype
LEVEL 2

Standards-based issuance

The institution can issue Open Badges 3.0 credentials, signed under its own identity, that anyone can verify.

Technically demonstrated · institutional identity still open
LEVEL 3

Credential ecosystem

The institution can issue, recognise and combine credentials from internal and external providers into a learner capability record.

Proposed direction

A credential ecosystem would support credentials issued by the institution, credentials issued by external learning partners, partner-delivered learning recognised by the institution, jointly designed or endorsed achievements, and — over time — a learner record that spans several providers. These are possible operating models, not adopted policy.

Pilot scenario · proposed

Trust boundary

What this demo does and does not claim.

Demonstrated here

  • A premium participant credential experience
  • A clean Open Badges 3.0 mapping of the record
  • Real cryptographic signing and independent verification
  • A DCC-compatible did:key and Data Integrity proof path
  • A five-step organiser issuance workflow

Not claimed

  • That University of Basel has adopted Open Badges
  • That Implement supports or has agreed to this pilot
  • That MIT or the DCC endorse this project
  • That any credential here is institutionally issued or signed
  • That the pilot models above are anything more than proposals

Everything signed in this repository is signed by a prototype demo issuer. Moving from a demo issuer to an institutional one is an organisational decision — issuer identity, key custody, issuance governance and revocation — not a remaining coding task.
