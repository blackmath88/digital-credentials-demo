01

Basel already has a credible certificate. It is just not portable.

Academic records are downloadable and can be authenticated through a university verification service. That is a strong baseline — and it is the thing the pilot is measured against, not the thing it replaces.

Today — PDF plus online verification

University of Basel, current state
  • Familiar, institutionally credible, accessible
  • Authenticity checkable through a university service
  • No standard machine-readable achievement model
  • Verification depends on the host remaining available
  • Competencies, criteria and evidence stay as pixels
  • Nothing the learner can take to another system

Proposed — the same certificate, plus a credential

Open Badges 3.0 over W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0
  • The certificate and PDF are retained, not replaced
  • Structured achievement, criteria, competencies and evidence
  • Signed under a Basel-controlled identity
  • Verifiable without trusting any Basel web page
  • Downloadable by the learner and readable by other tools
  • Revocable and correctable through a status list

The current model is kept as the pilot's control arm. The cohort receives both, so the comparison is measured rather than asserted.

03

One issuer. One partner. No dual signing.

Open Badges 3.0 models a single formal issuer, and multiple proofs do not create multiple issuing authorities. Partnership is expressed in the achievement, the branding and — optionally — a separate endorsement.

C Basel issues, achievement jointly definedImplement endorses, where it can Co-branded achievement, Basel as cryptographic issuer, optional separate endorsement credential from Implement. Recommended

“Shared” does not mean two cryptographic issuers. The standards-native pattern is one formal issuer, the partner named in achievement metadata, and a separately signed EndorsementCredential — which 1EdTech explicitly supports. If Implement is ready to operate its own organisational signing identity, it issues that endorsement. If not, the pilot begins with signed Basel issuance plus clearly governed co-branding, which is a legitimate starting point rather than a compromise.

05

The trust model, and where this prototype stops.

Every layer below is labelled with what this repository has actually demonstrated and what it has not. The gaps are institutional decisions or procurement tasks — which is why the next step is a decision rather than more code.

Institutional issuer authorityDecision · not built

Who may issue in the university's name. The pilot cannot start without an answer.

did:web or HTTPS issuer identityUniversity domain

A Basel-controlled identifier, resolvable without trusting our application. The prototype demonstrates the shape using did:key.

Signing key in managed KMS / HSMNamed owner · rotation · incident procedure

Not built here, deliberately. A demo key in a local directory proves the mechanism and nothing about custody.

OpenBadgeCredentialDemonstrated

Achievement, criteria, learning outcomes, assessment, workload, evidence policy, issue date and participant.

W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 proofDemonstrated

A real signature over the exact credential bytes. Altering one field breaks verification.

Status and revocationNot built

A privacy-preserving status list supporting revocation, suspension and correction — avoiding per-person designs that reveal who is checking a credential.

Human-readable page · signed file · PDFDemonstrated

Three renderings of one record. The public share page stays separate from the downloadable credential.

LinkedIn · wallet · independent verifierPartly demonstrated

LinkedIn handoff and an external verifier handoff exist. Wallet export against two independent tools is a pilot test, not a prototype feature.

Verified is not recognised.

Verification establishes authorship, integrity, conformance and current status. It does not establish that the educational claim is substantively true, and it does not create employer acceptance — a verifier still has to decide whether it trusts the issuer for that achievement.

This distinction is the single thing most likely to be misunderstood, and it belongs on every learner-facing surface. A credential that is cryptographically perfect and socially unread is still cryptographically perfect.

06

Why now — and why bounded.

W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 became a Recommendation in May 2025 and Open Badges 3.0 builds on it, with production implementations available. Swiss and European activity is real but fragmented — enough to justify learning by doing, not enough to join a settled ecosystem.

Reference pointWhat it demonstratesRelevance here

Badges for short continuing-education courses, with LinkedIn sharing, verification URL and wallet storage — on Open Badges 2.0.

The EDC route — related to, not interchangeable with, OB3

The interoperability fixture this prototype tests against

What the evidence does not support. Switzerland has emerging principles, not one binding national credential architecture — the swissuniversities position is explicitly not final, and there is no mature national portable-learning-record network to join. In Europe, Open Badges 3.0 and European Digital Credentials are not automatically interoperable: they use different application profiles, vocabularies and trust mechanisms, so a future EDC representation is a migration rather than a re-render. Open Badges 3.0 should not be described as “the European standard”.

07

What is in the pilot, and what is deliberately not.

The exclusions are as important as the inclusions. Most of them protect against claiming recognition the pilot cannot deliver.

In scope

  • One cohort, one non-credit achievement
  • Basel-controlled issuer identity and signing key
  • Open Badges 3.0 credential over W3C VC 2.0
  • Status list for revocation and correction
  • Public credential page, PDF and downloadable signed file
  • LinkedIn handoff and wallet export
  • Independent verification testing
  • The existing PDF process, retained as the control arm

Out of scope

  • ECTS, degree credit or qualification status
  • Automatic HR or hiring decisions
  • Blockchain — optional infrastructure, not required
  • Selective disclosure
  • Any dependency on Swiss e-ID before it is ready
  • Multi-university recognition claims
  • Institution-wide rollout
  • Dual cryptographic issuance

The pilot proves portable verification and governance. It does not announce a credential ecosystem, and it makes no recognition claim that a Swiss framework does not yet support.

08

What success looks like — and what would stop it.

One numeric target, because one is all the evidence supports. Everything else is instrumented and compared against the existing PDF process rather than scored against an invented threshold.

MeasureHow it is judged
Independent verifiability
100% of issued credentials pass an independent Open Badges 3.0 verifier. The only stated numeric target.
Failure cases
Altered, revoked and expired credentials behave correctly; key rotation leaves earlier credentials verifiable; a participant-name correction completes without breaking verification.
Portability
The credential verifies outside the issuing platform, in at least two independent tools.
Learner behaviour
Claim, download, LinkedIn-sharing and wallet-export rates — compared against PDF-only behaviour in the same cohort.
Learner understanding
Asked directly: do learners understand verified versus recognised? If not, that is a communication defect regardless of the technical result.
Operational effort
Issuance and correction time, support requests, and administrative effort against the current PDF process. Zero unresolved identity mismatches or key/status incidents.
Employer comprehension
Interviews. Measured as comprehension, not acceptance — acceptance is a policy and reputation decision, not something a pilot can produce.

Go / no-go. Continue only if all five hold: technical interoperability works; learners understand the credential; participant value is visible; operational effort is reasonable; governance is accepted. A clear “not yet, and here is exactly why” is a successful pilot outcome.

09

The first 90 days.

Governance first, because the binding constraints are organisational. The prototype in this repository is the test harness for the middle phase — not the production system.

DAYS 1–30

Governance and test definition

  • Nominate academic owner, credential registrar, privacy/security owner and Implement counterpart
  • Approve the achievement: outcomes, assessment, workload, evidence and explicit non-credit status
  • Choose Model C and draft the issuer / endorser wording
  • Write the procurement test: OB3 export, independent verification, status, key-control documentation, GDPR/FADP terms, exit portability

Exit: roles named, achievement approved, model chosen, procurement test written.

DAYS 31–60

Interoperability build

  • Compare Accredible, Open Badge Factory and one lightweight open-source route
  • Generate test credentials from this prototype
  • Validate with the 1EdTech verifier, VerifierPlus and two wallet/export pathways
  • Test key rotation, revoked, altered and expired credentials, and a participant-name correction

Exit: one route selected with evidence; all five failure cases pass.

DAYS 61–90

Live cohort

  • Enrol one Change Management cohort
  • Issue both the current-style certificate and the OB3 credential
  • Instrument claim, sharing, export, verification and support metrics
  • Interview learners and employers for comprehension
  • Produce the go/no-go dossier

Exit: dossier delivered, go/no-go decision taken.

The 90 days are a sequence, not a calendar promise. Legal review and institutional procurement can each exceed 30 days on their own, and the cohort phase cannot start until a cohort actually runs. Days 1–30 and 31–60 can overlap.

10

What has to be decided before anything is issued.

None of these is an engineering task. All of them require institutional review, and the pilot cannot begin without them.

Issuer authority
Who may issue in the university's name?
Key ownership
Can Basel legally and operationally control the signing identity and keys?
Participant data
Controller and processor roles, Swiss FADP assessment, data minimisation, retention and deletion, hosting jurisdiction.
Revocation and correction
Who may act, and what appeal process applies?
Hosting
Where do credentials, public pages and analytics live, durably?
Support ownership
Who answers a learner whose name is wrong?
Academic framing
Non-credit professional learning, or a formal microcredential? This changes everything downstream.
Implement's role
Will Implement operate an endorsement identity, or provide co-branding only?
Exit portability
Can credentials be exported with original signatures after a contract ends?
